Where generality meets fast decisions

General-purpose models can handle many tasks, but structured choices often need a faster interface. JEVal measures that capability; SII-Decision is an open model family built for it.

JEVal benchmark

JEVal puts diverse tasks into a shared decision interface: choose an option, make a binary judgment, or assign a score. Its ten domains test whether a model can move from evidence-based judgments to specialist knowledge, long-context understanding, and agent or tool choices without task-specific retraining.

SII-Decision

The First-Token Decision Readout restricts an open-weight LLM's next-token probabilities to valid option labels, producing a structured decision in one forward pass. Reasoning-to-Readout Self-Distillation then teaches this readout to match the distribution the same model reaches after reasoning, without gold labels or a stronger teacher.

Key results

From individual decisions to complete agent workflows and large-scale simulations, these results show where fast decision models work well and where they still struggle.

Decision quality on JEVal

The strongest decision models approach leading thinking LLMs on single-step accuracy. SII-Decision-27B reaches 79.24% accuracy and the lowest expected calibration error (ECE) of the 25 configurations. Across domains, decision models are strongest when the evidence is available in the input; the best generative LLMs retain an advantage in medicine, finance, and law.

Probability estimates need scrutiny

Jev often selects the most likely option on controlled probability tasks, yet its distribution is too concentrated. On prior items, it assigns the true mode 89.35% probability on average, compared with 35.60% in the known distribution. Ranking an option correctly does not ensure the probabilities are faithful.

Agent workflows: faster, with lower success

In τ-bench, Jev selects the next tool or response action while the same base LLM generates arguments or a user-facing answer. The median episode becomes shorter, but success falls and the observed P95 time rises. A fast local decision can still lead to a less reliable multi-step workflow.

Social simulation at scale

On 277,500 SocioBench respondent–question pairs, SII-Decision-27B attains 39.87% accuracy at 8.36 seconds per item, close to thinking DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 at 40.41% and 211.65 seconds. In ElectionSim, about 330,000 simulated voters yield correct calls for 47 of 51 states and 12 of 15 battleground states. Vote-share errors are about twice those of the original generative pipeline, and the simulation overestimates the Democratic share.
