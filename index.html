General Decision Models:
Benchmarking and Insights Beyond Jev

General intelligence and decisional intelligence meet in general decision models.

Abstract

General decision models, such as Jev, have recently emerged as efficient alternatives to LLMs for structured judgment and selection. But what kinds of decisions can these models reliably make, and how does their behavior change when individual decisions are composed into larger systems? To study this, we introduce JEVal, a bilingual benchmark comprising 11,257 instances from 36 datasets across 10 application domains, and evaluate 25 model configurations spanning general decision models and generative LLMs. Our results show that (1) general decision models are most competitive when decisions can be resolved from available evidence, but weaken when they require specialist knowledge or faithful uncertainty estimation: they can often identify the most likely outcome while substantially overstating its probability. (2) In more dynamic and realistic systems involving long-horizon, multi-step interactions, the advantages of fast local decision making are offset by reliability failures at the system level. on τ-bench, faster local decisions reduce median episode time but lower task success as decision errors accumulate over long trajectories. (3) In large-scale social simulation, decision models approach strong generative LLMs on individual response prediction at substantially lower inference cost, yet remain weaker in user profiling and exhibit larger aggregate estimation errors and systematic bias. Finally, we propose InnerJev-4B and InnerJev-27B, which internalize an open-weight LLM's own reasoning into a single-pass first-token decision through Reasoning-to-Readout Self-Distillation, with InnerJev-27B performing on par with Jev on JEVal while answering a typical query in about 0.1 s.

JEVal benchmark

JEVal puts diverse tasks into a shared decision interface: choose an option, make a binary judgment, or assign a score. Its ten domains test whether a model can move from evidence-based judgments to specialist knowledge, long-context understanding, and agent or tool choices without task-specific retraining.

Experimental results

We evaluate isolated decisions, controlled probability questions, interactive agents, and large-scale social simulations.

Decision quality on JEVal

The strongest decision models approach leading thinking LLMs on single-step accuracy. InnerJev-27B reaches 79.24% accuracy and the lowest expected calibration error (ECE) of the 25 configurations. Across domains, decision models are strongest when the evidence is available in the input; the best generative LLMs retain an advantage in medicine, finance, and law.

Probability estimates need scrutiny

Jev often selects the most likely option on controlled probability tasks, yet its distribution is too concentrated. On prior items, it assigns the true mode 89.35% probability on average, compared with 35.60% in the known distribution. Ranking an option correctly does not ensure the probabilities are faithful.

Agent workflows: faster, with lower success

In τ-bench, Jev selects the next tool or response action while the same base LLM generates arguments or a user-facing answer. The median episode becomes shorter, but success falls and the observed P95 time rises. A fast local decision can still lead to a less reliable multi-step workflow.

Social simulation at scale

On 277,500 SocioBench respondent–question pairs, InnerJev-27B attains 39.87% accuracy at 8.36 seconds per item, close to thinking DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 at 40.41% and 211.65 seconds. In ElectionSim, about 330,000 simulated voters yield correct calls for 47 of 51 states and 12 of 15 battleground states. Vote-share errors are about twice those of the original generative pipeline, and the simulation overestimates the Democratic share.

InnerJev

The First-Token Decision Readout restricts an open-weight LLM's next-token probabilities to valid option labels, producing a structured decision in one forward pass. Reasoning-to-Readout Self-Distillation then teaches this readout to match the distribution the same model reaches after reasoning, without gold labels or a stronger teacher.
