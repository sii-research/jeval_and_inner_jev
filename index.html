Benchmarking and Insights Beyond Jev

Abstract

General decision models such as Jev offer an efficient way to make structured judgments and selections. We ask which decisions they can make reliably and what happens when those decisions become part of larger systems. JEVal is a bilingual benchmark of 11,257 instances from 36 datasets in 10 domains; we use it to evaluate 25 decision-model and generative-LLM configurations. Decision models are most competitive when the relevant evidence is in the input, but struggle more with specialist knowledge and faithful probability estimates. In τ-bench, faster local choices shorten the median agent episode while reducing task success. In social simulation, decision models approach strong LLMs on individual survey prediction at lower inference cost, yet still show profiling gaps, aggregate estimation errors, and bias. Finally, we introduce InnerJev-4B and InnerJev-27B: open models that distill their own reasoning into a single-pass first-token decision.

JEVal benchmark

JEVal puts diverse tasks into a shared decision interface: choose an option, make a binary judgment, or assign a score. Its ten domains test whether a model can move from evidence-based judgments to specialist knowledge, long-context understanding, and agent or tool choices without task-specific retraining.

Experimental results

We evaluate isolated decisions, controlled probability questions, interactive agents, and large-scale social simulations.

Decision quality on JEVal

The strongest decision models approach leading thinking LLMs on single-step accuracy. InnerJev-27B reaches 79.24% accuracy and the lowest expected calibration error (ECE) of the 25 configurations. Across domains, decision models are strongest when the evidence is available in the input; the best generative LLMs retain an advantage in medicine, finance, and law.

Probability estimates need scrutiny

Jev often selects the most likely option on synthetic probability tasks, yet its distribution is too concentrated: on prior items, it assigns the true mode 89.35% probability on average, compared with 35.60% in the known distribution. Ranking an option correctly does not ensure the probabilities are faithful.

Agent workflows: faster, with lower success

In τ-bench, Jev selects the next tool or response action while the same base LLM generates arguments or a user-facing answer. The median episode becomes shorter, but success falls and the observed P95 time rises. This illustrates how a fast local decision can still lead to a less reliable multi-step workflow.

Social simulation at scale

On 277,500 SocioBench respondent–question pairs, InnerJev-27B attains 39.87% accuracy at 8.36 seconds per item, close to thinking DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 at 40.41% and 211.65 seconds. In ElectionSim, about 330,000 simulated voters yield correct calls for 47 of 51 states and 12 of 15 battleground states. Yet vote-share errors are about twice those of the original generative pipeline, and the simulation overestimates the Democratic share.

InnerJev

The First-Token Decision Readout restricts an open-weight LLM's next-token probabilities to valid option labels, producing a structured decision in one forward pass. Reasoning-to-Readout Self-Distillation then teaches this readout to match the distribution the same model reaches after reasoning, without gold labels or a stronger teacher.
