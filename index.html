General Decision Models:
Benchmarking and Insights Beyond Jev

Abstract

Many applications need a decision rather than a long generated response. We study general decisional intelligence: the ability to make structured choices across tasks and domains without task-specific retraining. Our bilingual benchmark, JEVal, contains 11,257 instances from 36 datasets across 10 domains. It evaluates 25 model configurations on decision accuracy and probability quality, then tests how local decisions behave in agent workflows and social simulation. We also introduce InnerJev-4B and InnerJev-27B, open models that distill reasoning into a first-token decision readout.

JEVal benchmark

One interface for choice, binary, and score decisions across knowledge, reasoning, tools, long context, and applied domains.

Experimental results

Strong single-step decisions are possible, but confidence and system-level behavior need separate evaluation.

Decision quality on JEVal

InnerJev-27B reaches 79.24% accuracy and the lowest expected calibration error (ECE) in the 25-configuration comparison. It is close to the leading thinking LLMs, while specialist domains such as medicine, finance, and law remain harder for decision models.

Probability estimates need scrutiny

Jev often selects the most likely option on synthetic probability tasks, yet its distribution is too concentrated: on prior items, it assigns the true mode 89.35% probability on average, compared with 35.60% in the known distribution. Ranking an option correctly does not ensure the probabilities are faithful.

Agent workflows: faster, with lower success

In τ-bench, Jev chooses the next tool or response action while the same base LLM supplies arguments or a user-facing answer. Median episode time falls, but end-to-end success falls too.

Social simulation at scale

On 277,500 SocioBench respondent–question pairs, InnerJev-27B attains 39.87% accuracy at 8.36 seconds per item, close to thinking DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 at 40.41% and 211.65 seconds. In ElectionSim, it predicts 47 of 51 state winners and 12 of 15 battleground winners; vote-share errors and directional bias still limit aggregate fidelity.

InnerJev

A first-token readout turns a language model's option scores into a decision. Reasoning-to-Readout Self-Distillation teaches that readout to approximate the model's answer after reasoning.
