01 / THE IDEA

From general intelligence
to general decisions.

Language models adapt to many tasks through natural language. Yet many real systems ultimately need a choice: classify an input, rank candidates, verify a claim, or select the next action.

We call the ability to make effective structured decisions across tasks and domains, without task-specific retraining, general decisional intelligence. Jev explores this space; our work asks where its capabilities hold and where they break.

03 / WHAT WE LEARNED

Fast decisions have
real boundaries.

01 / LOCAL DECISIONS

Strong when evidence is in view.

Leading decision models are competitive on tasks whose evidence is provided in the input. Specialist knowledge and faithful uncertainty estimation remain harder.

02 / AGENT WORKFLOWS

Faster steps do not guarantee a better journey.

In long-horizon agent tasks, faster local decisions reduce median episode time, while task success can fall as errors accumulate.

03 / SOCIAL SIMULATION

Scale exposes different errors.

Decision models can support large simulations at lower inference cost, but user profiling and aggregate estimates still show meaningful gaps and bias.

These findings summarize the analyses in the paper ↗.

04 / THE OPEN MODELS

Introducing InnerJev.

Can an open-weight language model turn its own reasoning into a fast first-token decision?

1 Read the scores of valid answer labels at the first answer token.

2 Let the model reason, then use its final answer distribution as a teacher.

3 Distill that distribution into a single-pass decision readout.
